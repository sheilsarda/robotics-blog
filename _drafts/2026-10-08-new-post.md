You have two piles of egocentric video. The first holds 1,000 hours from a head-mounted stereo rig with an IMU, calibrated, so visual-inertial SLAM gives you a metric, gravity-aligned camera trajectory, dense metric depth, and hand tracking you can trust. The second holds 100,000 hours of monocular RGB from a single camera with known intrinsics and nothing else. The second pile is a hundred times larger and carries the diversity: more people, more kitchens, more objects, more ways to hold a cup. The question to settle before anyone writes a pipeline: which quantities does the stereo pile measure that the mono pile has to guess, and for each one, do you recover it on mono by training a model, by installing a prior, or by measuring the error and filtering? The answer decides what the 1,000 hours are for.

The cost of guessing wrong has a number. [HumanEgo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24934), from May 2026, trains a robot policy from 30 minutes of Aria glasses video per task and reaches 92.5% success across four real tasks with no robot data. In an appendix the authors hold the demonstrations, the architecture and the training recipe fixed and swap only the hand tracker that produces the action labels. Aria's stereo-plus-IMU tracker gives 95% on the serve-bread task over 40 trials. WiLoR, a monocular tracker, gives 45%. HaMeR gives 32.5%. MediaPipe gives 0%. The authors trace the collapse to a 5 to 11 cm systematic depth offset from monocular scale ambiguity, which lands in the policy's reference frame and leaves it no consistent grasp to learn. Jitter and dropout finish the job: HaMeR's gripper-midpoint jerk runs an order of magnitude above WiLoR's, and MediaPipe finds hands in 66.5% of frames against WiLoR's 86.9%, while flattening the hand to about 7 cm thick where Aria measures 16. Those centimeters are the gap the 1,000 stereo hours have to close on the 100,000 mono hours.

## Stereo plus IMU measures four things that mono guesses

A calibrated stereo rig with an IMU measures metric scale from its baseline, measures gravity and short-term motion from the IMU, fuses both into a drift-corrected trajectory through a visual-inertial SLAM such as [ORB-SLAM3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898) or Aria's Machine Perception Services, and triangulates hands from two views. Mono video has to infer all four. Monocular depth models output relative depth, and the metric variants learn their scale from whatever rigs labeled their training data. NVIDIA's [ViPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10934) estimates intrinsics, camera motion and dense depth from raw video and calls its depth "near-metric", which is the honest word. Gravity comes from image cues. Drift accumulates with no inertial measurement to correct it. Hands come from one view and inherit the depth ambiguity. Against those four gaps the stereo set works as three tools: a teacher, a prior, and a ruler.

## Teacher: label the rig's own RGB stream and train mono models on it

The rig records an RGB camera next to its SLAM cameras. Aria Gen 1 carries an RGB camera at 30 fps alongside two monochrome SLAM cameras and two IMUs sampled at 1,000 Hz and 800 Hz. The [Aria Gen 2 pilot dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16134) records four CV cameras at 30 fps and 512x512, dual IMUs at 800 Hz, and RGB at 10 fps and 2560x1920. The gold set therefore contains what the mono fleet produces, single-camera RGB frames, and pairs each one with metric labels. Run the stereo pipeline once, keep the metric depth, the camera pose and the hand state for each RGB frame, and fine-tune the monocular estimators on those pairs.

[Depth Anything](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10891) set the pattern for depth in 2024 by having a teacher label 62 million unlabeled images, and [Depth Anything 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647) repeats it with a teacher-student scheme that beats VGGT by 44.3% on camera pose accuracy. The closest precedent for egocentric hands is [MINT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04958), from September 2026. Its EgoPipeline chains GeoCalib for intrinsics, MoGe-2 for a metric depth prior, MegaSaM or DROID-SLAM for the camera trajectory with that prior injected, and HaWoR for hands, then filters and smooths. The pipeline labeled 1,729 hours of Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS and EgoDex, kept 1,021 hours after dropping clips with no hands for more than a second or more than two hands, and trained one model that outputs camera trajectory, field of view, hand states and hand observability. The student runs 3.67 times faster than its teacher and reaches 13.6 mm hand error on HOT3D.

Read MINT's recipe for where the metric scale comes from. The teacher takes its scale from MoGe-2, a monocular network, and the authors then fine-tune on a small in-house corpus recorded with a single rig to correct the camera trajectory. That fine-tune is the job your 1,000 hours do, at a thousand times MINT's in-house scale. The stereo-plus-IMU measurements replace the monocular depth prior as the source of truth, and the student learns scale from a measurement instead of from another network's guess.

One gap to design around: the rig's RGB camera and the fleet's cameras differ in field of view, resolution and shutter. MINT and GeoCalib both predict field of view from the image rather than assuming it, and the training set needs crops, rescales and intrinsics perturbations wide enough to cover the fleet. The ruler section below tells you whether that worked.

## Prior: measure the constants that fix the gauge

Some quantities you do not want a network to regress per frame. You want one number per person or per scene that pins the scale. The wearer's hand width, the camera's height above the table, the gravity direction relative to the forward camera axis, and the sizes of objects that recur across the fleet. HumanEgo's MediaPipe failure, a hand squashed to 7 cm, shows what happens when none of these constants constrain the estimate.

The literature uses three different gauges. [TRAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17346) robustifies SLAM against moving people and takes its scale from the scene background. MINT injects a monocular depth prior. [MEgoVista](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16684), from September 2026, takes its metric gauge from calibrated stereo at initialization so that policies receive physical units, and scores the result inside a motion-capture volume against independent optical capture. Your gold set lets you build the gauge for your own domain. A thousand hours of IMU data gives you the distribution of head pitch over a counter. A thousand hours of triangulated hands gives you hand width per subject. The first metric sim scenes you reconstruct from the gold set give you object sizes and the offset between a hand and the object it holds. Those measured distributions anchor the mono pipeline's scale, in place of a prior fit to internet video by someone else. This is where the notes' line about distilling into a sim environment to learn priors belongs: the sim built from the gold set is where you measure the constants, and the constants are what you ship to the mono side.

## Ruler: hold out a slice, run mono-only, measure the damage

Hold out 100 hours of the gold set. Run the whole mono pipeline on its RGB stream alone, with the SLAM cameras and IMU hidden, and compare against the stereo truth. Per clip you get a scale error, drift per meter traveled, the distribution of hand depth offsets, the detection rate, and the hand jerk. HumanEgo says 5 to 11 cm of depth offset ends a grasp; your threshold per task comes out of this held-out slice.

With the held-out slice you make two decisions that the teacher and the prior leave open. It tells you which quantities the fine-tuned student now gets right and which still need a prior bolted on, so you stop fine-tuning where the error floor sits at the rig's own noise. And it sets the filter for the 100,000 hours. MINT kept 59% of its hours. Your retention rate should come from the ruler, with a look at what the filter discards, because the value of the big pile is its tail, and a filter tuned on kitchens will throw away garages. Each mono clip that passes carries a confidence from the same held-out calibration, which decides whether it feeds a sim scene or a policy.

## The output per clip, with sim at the end of the line

A mono clip that passes the filter yields a metric camera trajectory, bimanual hand states in the world frame, body pose when the task needs it, object poses, and a reconstruction of the static scene. [HaWoR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02973) and MINT cover the hands, and [ACE-Ego-Hand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20308), from September 2026, repurposes a video diffusion model to keep tracking through occlusion, cutting hand error by 30% on ARCTIC and 40% on HOT3D, and by 46% to 61% once out-of-sight hands count. [EgoAllo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03665) recovers body motion from the head trajectory, and [ReViV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17790), from July 2026, reconstructs camera, gaze, body, hands and depth from a mono egocentric video in one feed-forward model. [FoundationPose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08344) gives object poses when you have a mesh, and segment-and-track covers the rest.

The scene reconstruction is the real2sim half. [SplatSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10161) and [RoboGSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11839) place Gaussian splats inside a physics engine and render policy training views from them, and [D-REX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01151), from March 2026, makes the real-to-sim-to-real loop differentiable for dexterous grasping. For the Isaac Lab Arena environments we build, the splat is the visual layer, and the recipe's metric scale decides whether the collision proxies line up with it. A 3% scale error on a 10 cm mug is 3 mm, inside a parallel gripper's tolerance. A 5 cm hand depth offset is a missed grasp. The ruler tells you which of those two regimes a clip sits in before you spend a GPU-hour on its splat.

## Where the recipe breaks

Hands and objects move, and the static-scene assumption inside structure-from-motion breaks with them. TRAM masks the moving human before running SLAM, and [MonST3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03825) estimates a pointmap per timestep so moving geometry stops corrupting the camera solve. Long clips drift. A 10-minute clip with no IMU and no loop closure wanders, and [MASt3R-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12392) closes loops at 15 fps without assuming a fixed camera model, which helps when the fleet's intrinsics vary. Occlusion hides hands, which is the ACE-Ego-Hand result above. Phone cameras bring rolling shutter and motion blur that the rig's global-shutter cameras kept out of the teacher's training set. [EgoVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07607) collects with an iPhone on a head strap at 1080p and 30 fps next to its stereo rigs, so that failure mode is already in a public dataset.

The compute bill sets the last constraint. ViPE runs at 3 to 5 frames per second on one GPU. At 4 fps, 100,000 hours of 30 fps video is 750,000 GPU-hours, or a month on a thousand GPUs. Sampling keyframes at 1 fps cuts that to 25,000 GPU-hours. MINT's 3.67x speedup over its own pipeline is the other lever: distill the pipeline into one model before you point it at the big pile.

## The counter-position: mono foundation models make the stereo set unnecessary

ViPE's authors annotated close to 96 million frames, spanning about 100,000 internet videos and a million generated clips, with camera poses and depth and no stereo rig anywhere. Depth Anything 3 beats VGGT by 44.3% on camera pose and 25.1% on geometry from public academic data alone. MINT reaches 13.6 mm hand error with a teacher built from monocular models. On that evidence a team could skip the rig and run the mono stack on the 100,000 hours tomorrow.

Two facts cut against it. ViPE's authors chose the word "near-metric", and the residual behind that word is the 5 to 11 cm HumanEgo measured, which ended 50 percentage points of real-world success. And MINT's authors, with the strongest mono teacher published, still fine-tuned on single-rig data to correct trajectories, while MEgoVista's authors chose calibrated stereo for the gauge and checked it against optical capture. The foundation models do the first 90% of the work, and the stereo hours buy the last centimeters, which is where a grasp closes or misses.

The opposite objection says to skip mono instead: Aria Gen 2 rolls out to qualified labs in 2026, EgoVerse's 1,362 hours from 2,087 demonstrators came from Aria and industry stereo rigs, and [EgoDex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11709) got 829 hours of tracked hands from Vision Pro. Collect stereo for everything. The 100,000 mono hours exist today and the phones that record more of them sit in pockets, so the ratio stays near a hundred to one. EgoVerse itself ran its transfer experiments on the stereo-rig data and reported no result for the phone data. The published record holds no comparison of phone-captured against rig-captured demonstrations, and that comparison is the gap this recipe exists to close.

## A prediction

By the end of 2027, a published ablation in the HumanEgo format will show a monocular hand tracker within 10 points of the stereo tracker on real-robot success, and that tracker will have been fine-tuned on data from a calibrated stereo-plus-IMU rig. If a tracker trained with no stereo or inertial supervision closes the gap first, the premise of this post is wrong and the thousand hours were only ever a ruler.
